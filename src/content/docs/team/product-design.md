Product & Design is responsible for understanding what to build, making it worth using, and driving it to delivery. The function spans three tracks: product, design, and project leadership. Product people own the problem — discovery, requirements, scope and the client relationship. Designers own the experience — clarity, usability and visual coherence. Product Leads own the delivery momentum on each project — standups, priorities, team coordination and external communication.

The function is led by **Anandu** (Partner, Product & Design).

## Product Ladder

The product track has two paths that converge at senior levels: **Product Analyst** (requirements and discovery) and **Product Lead** (project coordination and delivery). Both paths sit within the Product & Design function and share the same level framework.

Each level assumes everything in the levels below it.

### L1 — Associate Product Analyst

*Impact: individual task level*

- Gathers requirements through interviews, workshops and documentation under a senior's guidance
- Writes user stories with help — understands the format but needs direction on completeness and edge cases
- Documents meeting notes, decisions and open questions accurately and promptly
- Supports backlog grooming and sprint planning as an active participant
- Asks clarifying questions rather than making assumptions

### L2 — Product Analyst / Business Analyst

*Impact: individual / project level*

- Independently owns requirements for a module or small project end-to-end
- Writes user stories with clear acceptance criteria that developers and QA can work from without ambiguity
- Maintains the backlog and documents all decisions with their rationale — not just what was decided, but why
- Engages stakeholders directly to clarify requirements and resolve conflicting priorities
- Flags scope creep and ambiguity early; doesn't let unclear requirements reach development

### L3 — Senior Analyst / Product Lead

*Impact: project level*

- Owns discovery and scope definition for a complete project: workshops, requirements, sign-off
- Runs client workshops and requirements sessions independently — structures them, facilitates them, drives them to decisions
- **As Product Lead on a project:** owns the backlog priority, runs standups, is the external SPOC for project status, and handles day-to-day trade-offs
- Mentors L1–L2 analysts; reviews their user stories and gives specific feedback
- Handles scope change requests independently: assesses impact, communicates clearly, gets sign-off

### L4 — Product Manager

*Impact: multiple projects*

- Owns product direction across a complex or large engagement: strategy, scope, backlog and stakeholder management
- Manages senior client stakeholders directly — navigates competing priorities, difficult conversations, and changing requirements without escalating to a partner
- Handles pushback, scope disputes and commercial implications of requirement changes
- Sets the quality bar for requirements and delivery across the projects they touch
- Can act as Engagement Owner on standard projects

### L5 — Senior Product Manager

*Impact: multiple projects / company*

- Oversees several simultaneous engagements as Product Lead or Engagement Owner
- Mentors L1–L3 product team members; owns their growth and development
- Sets product practice standards across the firm: how we do discovery, how we write requirements, how we manage scope and delivery
- Measured by the quality of decisions and delivery across all their engagements

### L6 — Principal / Head of Product

*Impact: company level*

- Defines the firm's product practice: methodology, tooling, templates, hiring bar
- Trusted by clients as the most senior product voice in the firm
- Develops and stretches the L4–L5 layer
- Measured on the long-term product capability of the whole function

## Design Ladder

### L1 — Associate Designer

*Impact: individual task level*

- Produces screens and assets under close guidance from a senior designer
- Follows the established design system; does not invent new patterns without approval
- Iterates quickly based on feedback — treats revisions as part of the process, not a sign of failure
- Delivers files that are organised, named correctly, and ready for handover

### L2 — Designer

*Impact: individual / project level*

- Independently owns UI/UX for a module or small project
- Maintains and extends the design system within the bounds set by the senior designer
- Participates actively in design reviews: gives feedback, asks questions, defends decisions
- Delivers handover assets that developers can implement without frequent clarifications
- Communicates design intent to developers — explains why, not just what

### L3 — Senior Designer

*Impact: project level*

- Owns UX and visual direction for a complete project end-to-end
- Sets the design language for a project: patterns, components, interaction models
- Runs design reviews and workshops with clients independently
- Reviews L1–L2 work and gives specific, actionable feedback
- Spots usability and consistency issues before clients do

### L4 — Lead Designer

*Impact: multiple projects*

- Sets UX and visual direction across multiple concurrent projects (fractional: direction at kickoff, reviews at milestones)
- Mentors the design team; owns their growth and quality of output
- Handles design conversations with senior clients directly: explains trade-offs, defends decisions, manages feedback

### L5 — Senior Lead Designer / Design Manager

*Impact: multiple projects / company*

- Spans several active engagements as design lead and quality reviewer
- Mentors L1–L3 designers; sets design standards for the firm
- Measured by the quality and consistency of design output across all engagements

### L6 — Principal Designer / Head of Design

*Impact: company level*

- Defines the firm's design practice: methodology, tooling, standards, hiring bar
- Trusted by clients as the most senior design voice in the firm
- Develops and stretches the L4–L5 layer
- Measured on the long-term design capability and reputation of the whole function
