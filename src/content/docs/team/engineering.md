Engineering & QA is responsible for everything we build and the quality of what we ship. Engineers design, implement and maintain the software; QA engineers own test strategy, coverage and release confidence. Both disciplines work shoulder-to-shoulder on every project — quality is not a handoff at the end, it's a shared standard throughout.

The function is led by **Anand** (Partner, Engineering & Technology).

Before reading the ladder, understand the [Career Philosophy](/team/career-philosophy/) — specifically the concepts of Impact and Leverage that underpin every level below.

## Software Engineering Ladder

### L0 — Intern

*Impact: individual task level*

- Works on well-scoped, short-term tasks with close supervision
- Learns the codebase, tooling, and how professional software development actually works
- Asks questions freely — curiosity and honesty about what you don't know is expected at this level
- Produces work that is reviewed heavily; that's the point, not a reflection of failure
- An internship at Arillus is about discovering whether engineering is your function and whether Arillus is your place

### L1 — Associate Software Engineer

*Impact: individual / project level*

- Delivers well-defined tasks with guidance from a senior
- Writes working code that passes review; reviews frequently catch significant issues
- Learns the codebase, tooling, and team conventions — asks for help early rather than staying stuck
- Treats code review feedback as learning, not criticism

### L2 — Software Engineer

*Impact: individual / project level*

- Delivers features end-to-end from a written story — including tests, edge cases, and error handling — with reviews catching only minor issues
- Debugs own problems methodically before escalating
- Estimates own work with reasonable accuracy; flags scope changes early
- Begins reviewing L1 code and giving useful, specific feedback
- Communicates progress without needing to be chased

### L3 — Senior Software Engineer

*Impact: multiple projects*

- Owns a module or workstream end-to-end: design, implementation, testing and handover
- Designs solutions before implementing — considers edge cases, performance and security from the start
- Breaks down ambiguous requirements into concrete technical tasks
- Reviews code as a routine, not an exception; actively mentors L1–L2 engineers
- Can **Tech Lead a standard project**: runs technical standups, unblocks teammates, owns the technical relationship with the Product Lead and client
- Handles straightforward technical conversations with clients directly
- Raises concerns about scope, feasibility or quality early

> Engineers at L3 and above are capable of being **Group Leads** — taking on people leverage alongside their technical work. See [Career Philosophy](/team/career-philosophy/) and [Groups](/team/groups/).

### L4 — Staff Software Engineer

*Impact: multiple projects simultaneously*

- Tech-leads complex or large projects; owns architecture decisions within them
- Acts as fractional Architect/Reviewer for projects led by L3s — reviews approach at kickoff, checks in at milestones
- Handles difficult technical client conversations independently: pushback, trade-offs, hard constraints
- Raises the team's engineering bar through documented standards and patterns, not just personal output
- Identifies systemic problems across projects and drives solutions, not just workarounds

### L5 — Senior Staff Software Engineer

*Impact: company level*

- Provides technical oversight across the firm's entire portfolio of active projects
- Escalation point for the firm's hardest technical problems
- Owns cross-project decisions: technology choices, reusable components, shared libraries, engineering standards
- Measured by the capability and quality of engineering output across the whole organisation — not just their own work

### L6 — Principal Software Engineer

*Impact: company level, exceptional*

- Owns the engineering practice itself: how Arillus builds software, stack decisions that span all clients, the firm's technical reputation
- Trusted by clients and partners as the highest technical authority in the firm
- Rare. This level exists when the firm needs it, not on a fixed timeline.

## QA Ladder

### L1 — Associate QA Engineer

*Impact: individual / project level*

- Executes test plans written by others accurately and thoroughly
- Reports defects with clear steps to reproduce, expected vs. actual behaviour, and environment details
- Learns the project's domain, testing tools, and team conventions
- Asks for help early when a test case is ambiguous or a defect is hard to reproduce

### L2 — QA Engineer

*Impact: individual / project level*

- Independently owns testing for a small project: writes test plans from user stories, tracks coverage, manages regression
- Identifies gaps in requirements and raises them before development begins
- Communicates defect status and release confidence without being prompted

### L3 — Senior QA Engineer

*Impact: multiple projects*

- Defines test strategy from project kickoff: what to automate, what to manual test, where the risk sits
- Reviews L1–L2 test plans and gives specific, constructive feedback
- Leads automation efforts and sets the standard for how the project tests
- Handles quality conversations with clients directly

### L4 — QA Lead

*Impact: multiple projects / company*

- Sets quality standards across multiple projects and audits adherence
- Reviews test approaches at project kickoff; spot-checks coverage and quality at milestones
- Mentors L1–L2 QA engineers; owns their growth
- Identifies systemic quality issues across projects and drives process improvements firm-wide
